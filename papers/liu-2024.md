# Liu et al. 2024 — Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts

N. F. Liu, K. Lin, J. Hewitt, A. Paranjape, M. Bevilacqua, F. Petroni, P. Liang.
Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics (TACL), vol. 12, pp. 157–173, 2024.
doi:10.1162/tacl_a_00638 · [PDF](liu-2024.pdf)
Owner: Asia

## In short

Language models do not use long contexts uniformly: downstream performance follows
a characteristic **U-shaped curve** as a function of the position of relevant
information. Models achieve their highest accuracy when critical information is
located at the very beginning (primacy bias) or end (recency bias) of the input
context. Performance degrades severely when models must access information in the
middle of long contexts. Crucially, extended-context models (e.g. 16k, 100k) do
not alleviate this positional degradation. Prompting LLMs with the entire history
is therefore an active trade-off: providing more context increases reasoning
difficulty and can even yield lower accuracy than answering without context.

## Setup

- **Tasks:**
  1. *Multi-document QA:* Based on NaturalQuestions-Open (2,655 queries). Input
     contains a question, 1 target Wikipedia passage (≤100 tokens) with the answer,
     and $k-1$ distractor passages ($k \in \{10, 20, 30\}$ documents; ~2k to ~6k tokens),
     retrieved via Contriever fine-tuned on MS-MARCO (§2.1).
  2. *Synthetic Key-Value (KV) Retrieval:* A string-serialized JSON object with $k$
     key-value pairs (128-bit random UUIDs; $k \in \{75, 140, 300\}$; ~4k to ~16k tokens).
     The model must retrieve the value matching a specified UUID key (§3.1).
- **Controlled variables:** Input context length ($k$) and position of the target
  document / key-value pair within the context (from 1st to $k$-th position).
- **Models evaluated:**
  - *Decoder-only open models:* MPT-30B-Instruct (8k context, ALiBi), LongChat-13B (16k context, condensed RoPE).
  - *Decoder-only closed APIs:* GPT-3.5-Turbo (4k) and GPT-3.5-Turbo-16K (0613 version); Claude-1.3 (8k) and Claude-1.3-100K; subset on GPT-4 (8k).
  - *Encoder-decoder models (ablation):* Flan-T5-XXL (512 token training window), Flan-UL2 (2048 token training window) (§4.1).
  - *Base models (ablation):* Base MPT-30B (§4.3); Llama-2 (7B, 13B, 70B; base vs chat/RLHF, App. E).
- **Inference settings:** Greedy decoding (`temperature=0`). Accuracy measured
  by string inclusion / exact match of gold answers (§2.1).
- **Reference baselines:** *Closed-book* (0 input documents, relying on parametric
  memory) and *Oracle* (only the 1 relevant document provided).

## Key findings

1. **The U-shaped serial-position curve:** Accuracy is highest when relevant information
   is at the beginning (1st position) or end of the prompt, dropping sharply when
   in the middle (Fig. 1, Fig. 5).
   - In 20-document QA, GPT-3.5-Turbo drops from >70% accuracy at position 1 to
     ~54–55% in the middle (Fig. 5).
   - In 20- and 30-document setups, GPT-3.5-Turbo performance on middle-context
     facts drops **below closed-book performance** (56.1% vs ~54%) (Table 1, §2.3).
     Irrelevant context actively harms reasoning.
2. **Extended-context models do not solve positional bias:** Performance curves
   for GPT-3.5-Turbo (4k) and GPT-3.5-Turbo (16k) are virtually identical and
   superimposed on 10- and 20-document tasks (Fig. 5). Claude-1.3 and Claude-1.3-100K
   exhibit the exact same overlap. Expanding context capacity does not improve
   context utilization (§2.3).
3. **Retrieval degradation on synthetic data:** On the pure key-value UUID task,
   GPT-3.5-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo-16K, and MPT-30B-Instruct still show U-shaped
   degradation at 140 and 300 pairs, falling to ~40–50% accuracy in the middle
   (Claude-1.3 maintained near 100%) (Fig. 7, §3.2).
4. **Architectural impact (Encoder-Decoder vs Decoder-Only):** Encoder-decoder models
   (Flan-UL2, Flan-T5-XXL) are robust to position within their training sequence
   length (only 1.9% gap between best and worst positions for Flan-UL2 at 2048 tokens;
   Fig. 8). However, when extrapolated beyond training sequence length, they
   revert to the same U-shaped curve (§4.1).
5. **Query placement helps synthetic KV retrieval but not natural QA:** Placing
   the query both before and after the context restores near-perfect accuracy on
   synthetic KV pairs, but does not fix the U-shaped curve on multi-document QA
   (Fig. 9, §4.2).
6. **Instruction fine-tuning is not the root cause:** Non-instruction-tuned base
   models (MPT-30B, Llama-2 base) also suffer from positional degradation (Fig. 10,
   Fig. 16, §4.3). Smaller models (Llama-2 7B) show primarily recency bias, while
   larger models (13B, 70B) display both primacy and recency U-shapes (App. E).
7. **Retriever-reader saturation:** In open-domain QA on Wikipedia, reader accuracy
   saturates at ~20 documents (~58%), even though retriever recall continues rising
   up to 50 documents (>88%) (Fig. 11, §5). Supplying more context gives diminishing
   returns while inflating latency, compute, and energy.

## Limitations

- Focuses on single-turn multi-document QA and synthetic retrieval; does not measure
  iterative multi-turn dialogue with back-and-forth conversational dependencies.
- Measures accuracy and latency/token count only; **no GPU energy or hardware
  power metrics** were measured.
- Does not test KV-cache reuse mechanics, prefix caching, or context compression
  algorithms (e.g. rolling summaries or selective truncation).

## What it means for our project

- **Full history may not be the quality ceiling.** Liu et al. test
  multi-document QA and key–value retrieval, not chat, but the effect
  suggests that facts in the middle of a long history can be missed (our
  interpretation). So we measure the quality of full history instead of
  assuming it is perfect (RQ2, RQ3).
- **Context management can help quality, not only energy:** a window or a
  summary keeps the prompt short, but only if the needed fact survives.
- **Benchmark design:** plant facts in early (turns 1–3), middle (≈ 10–15 of
  40) and recent (last 2–3) turns. Asking only at the end would hide the
  U-shape.
- Expected failure modes per strategy (hypotheses):
  - *Full history:* long prompt, possible middle-position misses.
  - *Sliding window:* early facts gone; prefix changes every turn.
  - *Summaries:* keep a fact only if the summary does; extra decode energy.
